# Start with the candidate’s experience

Use this for the first conversation or a change in direction. Continue from records/STATE.md when available. Work in small batches and skip questions the candidate has already answered. Give them a useful result while the rest of the profile is still taking shape.

## First exchange

If setup is incomplete, use [the setup guide](setup-codex.md). Otherwise, review the supplied career handoff or résumé briefly and ask two questions that decide where to start:

1. What kind of work would you like to do more of in your next job? If titles are hard to pick, tell me which tasks you like and which you want less of.
2. Where would you seriously consider working? Which of onsite, hybrid, remote or relocation are on the table?

Use their answers to guide the next exchange. For example, if they are deciding between two directions, show how the work differs and ask which they want to explore first. Treat examples as options. Let them leave a direction undecided.

## Turn the answers into a usable profile

- Bring in only relevant work history, qualifications and examples from their corrected handoff, résumé or statements. For each material fact, record the source, status and date as available. Reported and accepted by the candidate is distinct from independently corroborated. Keep contradictions open until they resolve them. Preserve the corrected version for future chats.
- Find out what matters in practice. Ask about the work they want to own, industries or employers they prefer or wants held, acceptable locations and remote eligibility, travel, shift, pay and other practical limits when relevant. Record each as a requirement, preference or open question. A target pay range is not permission to disclose it. An unanswered item is not a constraint or a commitment.
- Ask how they want the assistant to help. For example, do they want blunt fit assessments or a more conversational explanation? A short shortlist or more room to explore? Should it suggest stretch roles? Let them change the defaults at any time.
- Show a compact recap of the profile and search plan for correction. A response like “start with those roles” can establish search scope. Keep profile corrections and application authority separate.

Start records from the templates as needed. Keep their corrected ChatGPT recap in records/CAREER-HANDOFF.md. Store only work-related material that they choose to bring forward. Give them copyable notes when the current chat cannot write the files.

## Build the experience log as you work

Ask for one example at a time. Pick something specific, such as a problem they solved, an improvement they suggested, something other people relied on them for, or work they had to learn fast. Use their own words first. Follow with the questions that matter for this example.

- What was happening and what were you responsible for?
- What did you notice, decide, make or change? What did others contribute?
- What tools or techniques did you actually use? How did you check the result?
- What happened next? Which outcomes or numbers do you have evidence for?

Reflect back the skill and judgment you can explain from the example. If they say “I just fixed it,” ask how they narrowed it down and checked that it stayed fixed. You might then say, “You isolated the cause, made the correction, and checked that the equipment behaved as expected. That's a troubleshooting example. What did you do to check it?” Use their real details and offer a draft they can adjust. Ask before putting a provisional story into a résumé.

Look for patterns across examples they confirm, such as people trusting them with awkward problems, steady troubleshooting, teaching colleagues or following through on a build. Use them in the experience log, positioning and interview practice when relevant. Credit team work accurately. Accept qualitative results when numbers are unavailable. A project, title or rejection cannot tell you everything about what they are capable of.

Keep sensitive or employer-confidential details out of candidate-facing outputs. Use a job's real requirements to prompt occasional useful follow-up, without asking the same question repeatedly or turning the conversation into a test.
